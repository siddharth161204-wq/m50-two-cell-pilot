# Pilot B decisions

Every analysis choice that is not fixed by the brief is recorded here in full sentences, with its reason, before the runs it affects.

## Inputs

D1. The two-sigma band edges come from Figure 7 of Koenig, Zhao and Deng, Chemical Engineering Journal 507 (2025) 160402, measured from the embedded figure image by locating the y-axis ticks and the bar outlines (`inputs/measure_deng_fig7.py`). The current-study bars read 15.82 % of the mean for T_initial, 16.64 % for T_onset and 9.15 % for T_max, each to about 0.1 percentage point. The scripts use 15.8 % and 16.65 %; the differences from the measured values move no band edge by more than 0.02 K. The span of 9 % to 17 % given in the paper's text therefore runs from T_max at the low end to T_onset at the high end. The brief's provisional assignment (17 % to T_initial, 9 % to T_onset) is not what the figure shows and is not used.

D2. The bar height is read as the full width of the plus or minus two-sigma interval, expressed as a percentage of the mean in degrees Celsius, the unit in which every metric in the paper is reported. The paper names what the bars compare: "the 4σ uncertain ranges". The bands are centred on the Table A.1 main-cluster means, because the paper centres the Figure 7 means on zero. T_initial: 183.5 C with a band of 169.0 to 198.0 C. T_onset: 278.9 C with a band of 255.7 to 302.1 C.

D3. The jelly-roll properties are recomputed from the PyBaMM "ORegan2022" parameter set (PyBaMM 26.9.0.0, `inputs/oregan2022_properties.py`) with half current-collector thicknesses per electrode pair. They reproduce the brief: density 2938.7 kg/m3, specific heat 842, 999 and 1110 J/kgK at 25, 60 and 100 C, through-layer conductivity 1.23 to 1.38 W/mK, in-plane conductivity 24.9 to 25.1 W/mK, roll volume 1.918e-5 m3. The component specific-heat fits turn non-physical above about 150 C (the positive-electrode value is negative at 200 C), so no temperature-dependent cell heat capacity can be built from them over the range this pilot needs. Both models therefore use the brief's constant cell-average value, 950 J/kgK on 65 g.

D4. The O'Regan et al. paper (Electrochimica Acta 425 (2022) 140700) is not open access (Crossref lists only Elsevier and STM licences), so the cross-check against its measured cell-level values is deferred. No number in this pilot depends on it.

## Physics shared by both models (decided by Sid, 7 October 2026)

D5. Radiation between the facing cans uses the view factor of Incropera's formula for parallel equal cylinders, F = 0.160 at a 2 mm gap, which is defined per unit of the whole lateral area of a cylinder. The script as received multiplied it by a quarter of the lateral area, which understates the path by a factor of four. Both models now use the grey two-surface radiosity result with the room as a black third surface, to first order in (1 minus eps) F: cell 2 gains eps^2 sigma F A_lateral (T1^4 minus T_room^4) and loses eps sigma A_outer (T2^4 minus T_room^4) to the room over its whole outer area. In the lumped script this is written as an exchange eps^2 sigma F A_lateral (T1^4 minus T2^4) plus a room loss over A_outer minus eps F A_lateral, which is algebraically the same. In the resolved model the gain is spread over the lateral surface with the exact local view factor from each surface point to cell 1 (two-dimensional, infinite cylinders), whose integral is F A_lateral.

D6. Room convection acts on the outer area of each cell minus the quarter of the lateral area that faces the other cell across the 2 mm gap, in both models, because air in a 2 mm gap conducts rather than convects and that path is already G_gap. The convective area is 4.184e-3 m2 instead of 5.346e-3 m2. The strip contact patches keep their room losses, because the strips are thin and exposed, which keeps the lumped model identical for every strip arrangement.

D7. The resolved cell 2 takes the two-strip arrangement of the brief's resolved section for all 42 cases: a positive strip on an 8 mm disc at the centre of the top cap and a negative strip on the bottom annulus from 15 mm diameter to the can rim, each carrying half of G_bus through a Robin condition with coefficient G_strip divided by the patch area. Seven extra runs at the median energy and tau_rel = 30 s put the full G_bus through the negative-rim annulus alone, which is the series-string arrangement of the brief's design section. The lumped model is the same for both arrangements.

D8. The predictions commit is pushed to a public GitHub repository before any coupled resolved run, so the order of prediction and result can be checked by a reader.

## Resolved model construction

D9. The resolved cell 2 is the brief's cylinder of diameter 21.1 mm with the full can length of 70.1 mm, so that its outer, lateral, end and facing areas are exactly those the lumped node uses. The cap assembly, headspace and mandrel hole are not resolved.

D10. Both models hold the same total heat capacity, C = 65 g x 950 J/kgK = 61.75 J/K, which V1 requires. The steel can is a 0.25 mm shell on every outer surface with in-plane conductivity 15 W/mK and a heat capacity of 9 g x 500 J/kgK = 4.5 J/K spread uniformly over the outer area. The roll carries the remainder as a uniform volumetric heat capacity, 57.25 J/K over the cylinder volume. Putting the brief's 2939 kg/m3 at 1000 J/kgK into the whole cylinder would hold 72 J/K, 17 % more than the lumped node, and V1 would fail by construction.

D11. Roll conductivity is radial 1.3 W/mK, axial and azimuthal 25 W/mK, constant, as the brief states.

D12. Only half of cell 2 is meshed. The plane through both cell axes is a plane of symmetry for every boundary condition, so it carries zero flux.

D13. Time stepping is backward Euler at 0.1 s with a row-sum (lumped) mass matrix. Room radiation is taken from the previous step, which is stable here by three orders of margin. Cell 1's temperature history is written by the lumped script every 0.1 s from t = 0 on an integer step grid, so the resolved model reads T1 at its own step times without interpolation. The brief asked for every 0.5 s; the finer series removes one source of difference between the models.

D14. Reported per case and per band edge: the first crossing time of the local maximum over all nodes and where that maximum is; the first crossing time of the heat-capacity-weighted mean temperature, which is the quantity a lumped node represents; and the local maximum minus that mean at the moment the local maximum crosses. Crossing times are interpolated linearly within the step. Where the maximum is, is reported two ways. The location label used for scoring and for the figure is the path that heats the hottest node directly at that moment: "terminal end" when the strip input to that node exceeds the air-gap plus radiation input, "gap-facing surface" otherwise, and "elsewhere" when the node receives neither. This rule is needed because the negative strip's annulus runs to the can rim, so the bottom edge of the gap-facing surface receives both paths and a purely geometric label would call a gap-driven hot spot a terminal end. The geometric description is reported alongside: positive terminal end (within 3 mm of the cap face and within 7 mm of the axis), negative terminal end (within 3 mm of the bottom face and outside 4.5 mm radius), the gap-facing rim of the negative end (the part of the latter within 2 mm of the can wall and within 60 degrees of the line of centres), gap-facing surface (within 2 mm of the can wall and within 60 degrees of the line of centres) and elsewhere, with the node's radius, angle and height.

## Verification design (fixed before any coupled resolved run)

D15. Checks that carry no information about the coupled cases run before the predictions are written: V0, the flux identity, evaluates every resolved boundary operator on a uniform temperature field and compares its total heat flow with the lumped formula for the same path (acceptance within 0.1 % for every path); V1a, pure conservation, runs both models with no paths, no room losses and the same uniform internal heat input (acceptance: resolved mean equals the lumped node to 0.01 % of the rise); and analytic checks of the anisotropic conduction against the steady radial and axial solutions.

D16. V1 as the brief defines it runs after the predictions are public, because it uses cell 1's temperature history from the median case and so carries information about the coupled cases. With the busbar strips and the air-gap conduction switched off, cell 2 is heated only by radiation from cell 1. Under D5 that gain depends on T1 alone, so both models receive exactly the same heat, and any difference between the resolved mean and the lumped node comes from the room losses being evaluated on the local surface temperature instead of the mean. Acceptance: within 1 % of the lumped node's rise above ambient at every time, as the brief requires, together with the resolved model's own energy account (stored heat against heat in minus heat out, integrated over the run) closing within 0.1 %.

D17. V2 and V3 use the median energy, tau_rel = 30 s and G_bus = 0.05 W/K, the middle of the conductance sweep, and run only after the predictions commit is public.

## Battery Failure Databank (retrieved 7 October 2026)

D18. The Databank (revision 2, February 2024; file hash in inputs/databank/SOURCE.txt) has no state-of-charge column. Its ReadMe defines the pre-test open-circuit voltage, and the LG 21700-M50 (BV) tests fall into three clean groups by that voltage: 4.16 to 4.19 V, 3.82 to 3.83 V and 3.44 to 3.45 V. The 100 % SOC tests are those at or above 4.1 V. One test, DLS19_Feb_Run089, has no recorded voltage and is excluded because its state of charge cannot be confirmed; including it would move the median cell-body energy from 21.50 to 21.69 kJ and leave the minimum and maximum unchanged.

D19. The three E_BODY values are the minimum, median and maximum of the column Energy-Fraction-Cell-Body-kJ over the 17 tests at 100 % SOC: 8.75, 21.50 and 33.22 kJ. The ReadMe defines that column as follows: "Fraction of the total energy released through the cell casing." It is a share of the corrected total energy yield. They rest on 7 heater tests without the internal-short device (8.75 to 21.89 kJ, median 11.54) and 10 nail tests (18.67 to 33.22 kJ, median 25.88). The Databank holds no internal-short-device tests for this cell, so the brief's third trigger type is absent. The median value belongs to an actual test (SPR2021_ESTA_8B100-01_SOC_RUN197, nail). Across the 17 tests the corrected total energy yield is 69.2 to 110.8 kJ and the cell body carries 10.2 to 31.1 % of it, so the cell-body energies are well below the script's placeholders of 20, 35 and 50 kJ.

D20. Both models keep the brief's cell heat capacity, 65 g at 950 J/kgK. The Databank records a pre-test mass of 68.05 to 68.52 g for these cells; the 3.4 g difference (cap assembly, tabs, wrap, mostly metal at about 500 J/kgK) would add about 1.7 J/K, 3 %, to both models alike. Cell 1 keeps this intact heat capacity although the Databank tests eject 30 to 54 g of mass; that is part of the limit that the release is a Databank energy and not a measured heat-rate curve.

## Added after the predictions were public

D21. The median case of D17 (21.5 kJ, tau_rel 30 s, G_bus 0.05 W/K) crosses no band edge in either model, so V2 there can report only peak temperatures. V2 is therefore also run on the case that carries the first headline number, 33.2 kJ at tau_rel 30 s and G_bus 0.5 W/K, where both models cross: one mesh refinement (every spacing halved, 297,057 nodes) and one time-step halving (0.05 s). The same two checks are run on the 10 s release of that case, whose crossing at 5.7 s is the steepest in the sweep. Both V2 cases are reported.

D22. Runs use one MKL thread per process (26.8 ms per step against 47.2 ms with two threads on this machine) and two processes at a time. This changes nothing in the arithmetic beyond the order of floating-point operations inside the solver.

D23. Exploratory, not pre-registered: the onset criteria are whole-cell ARC thresholds, and the brief applies them to the resolved model's hottest point. To show how much material that point stands for, chosen cases are rerun recording the fraction of the cell volume at or above 169.0, 183.5, 198.0 and 255.7 C at every step (`src/hot_volume.py`). The physics and the numbers of the sweep are unchanged by this; it only adds an output. The fractions are recorded every 0.5 s, and the time at which a given fraction is first reached is interpolated linearly between records (`src/note_numbers.py`).

D24. To give the conductance sweep a physical scale in the note, two busbars are converted to conductance over the 23.1 mm pitch, centre to centre: a nickel strip of 0.2 by 8 mm section (k about 90 W/mK) gives 90 x 1.6e-6 / 0.0231 = 6.2 mW/K, and a copper plate of 30 mm2 section (k about 385 W/mK) gives 385 x 3.0e-5 / 0.0231 = 0.50 W/K. These are illustrations only: contact resistance and the real path length of a busbar change them, and no result depends on them.

D25. Exploratory, not pre-registered: two-way coupling. As the brief specifies, the registered runs drive the resolved cell 2 with the T1(t) history of the lumped pair, so cell 1 loses the heat that a lumped cell 2 would draw. The strip patches of the resolved cell 2 run hotter than its mean and draw less, so cell 1 should run hotter. `src/resolved_cell2.py` therefore has an option in which cell 1 is a lumped state inside the resolved time loop: it receives the same triangular release, integrated exactly over each step; it loses heat to the room as in the lumped script; it loses through the strips and the air gap exactly what the resolved cell 2 receives; and it gains back, by reciprocity, the radiation eps^2 sigma f A (T2^4 minus T_room^4) summed over the resolved surface. Four cases are rerun (`src/sensitivity.py --mode two-way`): 33.2 kJ at 500 mW/K with tau_rel 10 and 30 s, 21.5 kJ at 500 mW/K with tau_rel 30 s, and 33.2 kJ at 200 mW/K with tau_rel 30 s. A check (`src/check_two_way.py`) runs the coupled loop with the roll conductivity raised to 1e4 W/mK, where cell 2 is nearly isothermal, so the coupled pair must reproduce the lumped pair of `lumped_two_cell.py`.

D26. Exploratory, not pre-registered: isotropic control. To test whether the low through-layer conductivity of the jelly roll is what holds the strip heat at the terminal, the 33.2 kJ, tau_rel 30 s, 500 mW/K case is rerun with the through-layer conductivity raised to the in-plane value, 25 W/mK, and everything else unchanged (`src/sensitivity.py --mode isotropic`).

D27. The brief's figure has two panels: the crossing time of T_initial against busbar conductance, and the hot-spot location with the hottest-minus-mean temperature. These are panels b and c. Panel a, the peak temperature of each model against the two onset bands, is added so that the crossings can be read against how close each model comes to the bands. Panel b shows the maximum Databank energy beside the median, because at the median energy the lumped node never crosses 183.5 C and the panel would hold a single point.
